The science behind it

Every number. Explained.

How ForeCheck measures, how it calculates every number it shows, and the research behind each method. Written so you can show it to anyone: coaches, players, medical staff, club management.

The rule behind everything

ForeCheck never guesses

When a value is missing, ForeCheck shows it as missing. It never fills the gap with an estimate, and never puts a modelled number in place of a measured one. When a method is a club choice rather than a research finding, it says so.

Where the numbers come from

  • Sensor vest
  • Heart rate
  • Bike power
  • Tests and check-ins

No black box

No readiness or injury-risk score

Research does not support one number that predicts injury. ForeCheck shows what changed, against what, and how sure it is, and the staff decide.

What you see instead

  • What changed
  • Against his own normal
  • Is the data complete

01 · Session load

In ForeCheck

Volume and intensity

How much did the player do in this session, and how hard was it per minute on the ice?

How it is calculated

  1. Measured by the player's sensor vest: on-ice load (accelerometer load), explosive efforts, high-intensity load, active time on ice, skating strides and skating load.
  2. Daily volume = Skating Load: the peak force of each skating stride multiplied by the player's body mass, summed over the day.
  3. Daily intensity = Skating Load ÷ active minutes on ice. Active minutes, not the whole session, so players with little ice time are not made to look easier.
  4. These two drive the daily load, the ACWR and the 7-day plan forecasts built on them. Skating Load is also the volume on the session tiles and charts, so every screen uses the same measure. The session volume and intensity scores below use their own three-metric models.
  5. Totals over periods or days are summed first and divided once. ForeCheck never averages ratios.

Why

Skating Load counts only skating strides, and weighs each one by the player's body mass. A heavier player pushing the same stride does more work, and Skating Load reflects that, which is why an ice-hockey framework for choosing load metrics calls it the biologically valid volume measure.

On-ice load, the alternative, appears in one paper on professional hockey. It is the vest's total accelerometer load with the very smallest movements removed: it adds up every movement in every direction (stride, upper body, shooting, contact) and ignores the player's weight. The framework's criticism is that it mostly repeats that accelerometer total, whose formula is reported inconsistently in the literature, and that it under-counts defencemen, who glide and skate backwards more.

The earlier daily volume (0.4 × on-ice load + 0.4 × explosive efforts + 0.2 × high-intensity load) was a club choice with no published source. ForeCheck replaced it with Skating Load on 14 October 2026.

On our own season data the earlier mixed volume and Skating Load agree closely (r = 0.95), so the switch is a refinement, not an upheaval.

Limits, stated openly

Skating Load sees only skating. Off-ice work, such as the bike, is counted through heart-rate load instead, never mixed into skating volume.

02 · Every metric

In ForeCheck

Compared with the player's own normal

Was today hard or easy for this player, compared with what is normal for him?

How it is calculated

  1. The session is compared with the same player's earlier sessions of the same type: game days with game days, the day before a game with other days before a game.
  2. The session itself is never part of its own baseline.
  3. At least 3 earlier sessions are needed. Otherwise ForeCheck shows "insufficient baseline" instead of a weak comparison.
  4. z = (today − the player's mean) ÷ the player's standard deviation.
  5. Session volume score = 0.33 × z(Skating Load) + 0.33 × z(explosive efforts) + 0.33 × z(changes of direction).
  6. Session intensity score = 0.33 × z(Skating Load per active minute) + 0.33 × z(% high-force strides) + 0.33 × z(% high-intensity skating load).
  7. Each score is also shown as a T-score (50 + 10 × z), which coaches find easier to read.
  8. Classes run from very easy (z ≤ −1.5, advise a top-up) through normal (−0.8 to 0.8) to very hard (z ≥ 1.5, plan recovery).

Why

A z-score scales each player against himself, so a heavy defenceman and a light forward can be read on the same page.

Both scores, their equal weights, the T-scores and the class cut-offs with their actions follow the multi-modal models of a published framework for choosing load metrics in ice hockey. The three metrics in each score measure different movement qualities but are correlated.

In one elite men's team over a full season, games carried more load than practices on every measure, and time on ice alone was not an accurate measure of load. Absolute values depend on the sensor's settings, so ForeCheck compares each player with himself rather than with published numbers.

Limits, stated openly

Stated plainly: the weights and cut-offs come from the authors' professional practice and unpublished data. They have not been validated against injury or performance. Early in a season the history is short, so the comparison is less certain.

  2. Nightingale S, Hughes J, De Ste Croix M, Pfeifer C. Practice and match load characteristics of elite men's ice hockey. Int J Strength Cond. 2024;4(1). eprints.glos.ac.uk/14636

03 · Load over time

In ForeCheck

Acute and chronic load (ACWR)

How does recent load compare with what the player is used to?

How it is calculated

  1. Acute load is a short exponentially weighted moving average (EWMA), chronic load a long one. ACWR = acute ÷ chronic.
  2. It is calculated for volume (Skating Load), intensity (Skating Load per active minute) and heart-rate exertion. Days without a session count as zero load.
  3. Windows: 5-day acute and 20-day chronic, changeable in Settings. Ice-hockey practitioners report using a 5-day against a 20-day average to plan practice volume. That is professional practice, not a validated window, and they used plain rolling averages where ForeCheck uses EWMA. Team-sport EWMA studies more often use 7 and 28 days.
  4. The 0.8 to 1.3 band is a monitoring guide, never an injury threshold.

Why

EWMA gives recent days more weight than older ones. A plain rolling average treats a session 27 days ago the same as yesterday's. EWMA was proposed for exactly this reason, and in a two-season study of 59 elite Australian football players it was more sensitive than the rolling average to the higher injury risk at very high ratios.

Other researchers say the evidence for one averaging method is still thin, and that no study has shown that changing the ratio changes injury rates. So ForeCheck uses ACWR only to describe load, and never turns it into an injury prediction or a risk score.

Limits, stated openly

A ratio jumps when chronic load is low, for example after an injury or a break. Read it together with the absolute numbers.

04 · Data quality

In ForeCheck

Vest on, but on the bike

Did the player ride the bike with his sensor vest on, and is that distorting his skating numbers?

How it is calculated

  1. Pedalling-sized movement: in at least half the seconds of each minute the vest's accelerometer load is at least 0.02, and 95 % of seconds stay at or below 0.10. Skating and running move the vest far more.
  2. Heart rate up: the stretch averages at least 55 % of the player's maximum heart rate.
  3. No skating: the sensor's own stride detector counts at most 8 strides a minute. Skating gives 20 to 120.
  4. Long enough: at least 15 minutes, with pauses of up to 5 minutes joined.
  5. Not mixed with skating: only recordings that are mostly off-feet are flagged. Short spins inside a practice, and spins during games, are left alone.
  6. Likely or unsure: each listed session is marked likely off-ice (at most 2.5 skating strides and 4 accelerations a minute) or unsure, some skating detected. The limits come from the coach's first 21 decisions: every confirmed bike session stayed under both, every denied on-ice rehab session was clearly above. Unsure sessions are still listed, never dropped, and the limits are checked again as more decisions come in.
  7. Independent proof: when the player's Wattbike ride file is uploaded, ForeCheck shows the matching ride and its measured power.
  8. The coach decides. Confirm keeps the session's heart-rate load and removes its skating numbers from volume, intensity, skating ACWR, z-scores, team averages and alerts, with nothing put in their place. Deny changes nothing. Every decision is logged and can be undone.

Why

Players often wear the vest on the bike or rower so heart rate is recorded. Time on ice then stays tiny while pedalling can trigger explosive efforts, so intensity jumps. In one rehab case, bike days read 46 to 61 efforts a minute against 25 to 35 on the same player's full practices. Left in, that inflated number sits in his chronic average and makes his real return to the ice look easier than it is.

Limits, stated openly

A recording can only be split by time when the staff mark a period in the sensor's own software. If the player also skated briefly in the same recording, that skating leaves with the bike part. The list shows how many minutes are involved before you decide.

05 · Conditioning

Measured load in ForeCheck · CP scoring in development

Bike load against the player's own threshold

How hard was this ride for this player, and how close did it come to what a game asks of him?

How it is calculated

  1. Heart-rate load is valid on the ice and on the bike, so it is the one load measure compared across both. Bike work is never converted into "ice units" and never merged with skating into one number.
  2. On the bike, power is measured and scored against the player's own critical power (CP), his aerobic ceiling, and W′, the fixed amount of work available above it, his sprint reserve.
  3. CP and W′ come from a test on the Wattbike: a 3-minute all-out test, or 3-, 7- and 12-minute maximal efforts. Until a player is tested, his rides show measured values only: kJ, average and peak watts.
  4. Rides can follow the player's own game: shift-length efforts sized as a share of his W′, a target of his measured game heart-rate exposure, and a comparison afterwards.

Why

Internal load (heart rate, perceived exertion) tracks movement load across team-sport sessions, but how strongly depends on the type of session. There is no fixed exchange rate between bike and skating work.

A single FTP number squeezes aerobic and anaerobic ability into one. For a power athlete, long blocks set from FTP end up above his real aerobic ceiling and sprints far below what he can produce. Setting long blocks from CP and sprints from W′ fixes both. How fast W′ comes back differs a lot between people, so each player is tested rather than given a population value.

Ice hockey is played in shifts of about 30 to 80 seconds with long recoveries, about 40 seconds of it effective play. Heart rate climbs toward maximum each shift and averages about 85 % of maximum on the ice, and practices reach less time above 90 % than games.

Limits, stated openly

The W′ model is only as good as each player's own test. ForeCheck does not work out CP from ordinary rides.

06 · Conditioning

In ForeCheck

Running and on-feet conditioning

How much running did the player do outdoors, how fast, and how hard was it on his heart?

How it is calculated

  1. An ice rink has no GPS signal, so every indoor recording shows 0 m of GPS distance. A recording with GPS distance is outdoor work. It goes to the same Confirm / Deny list as bike sessions: if confirmed, the skating numbers leave the hockey load and the heart-rate load stays.
  2. Measured by the vest: total distance, maximum speed, accelerometer load and heart-rate exertion.
  3. Calculated from the vest's 10 samples per second: distance in high-speed running (19.8 to 25.2 km/h) and sprinting (above 25.2 km/h), and accelerations and decelerations of at least 3 m/s² held for at least 0.5 s.
  4. Heart rate: minutes at or above 85 % and 90 % of maximum, and Edwards TRIMP, which counts the minutes in each 10 % band from 50 % to 100 % of maximum heart rate and weights them 1 to 5.
  5. Distance, high-speed plus sprint distance and TRIMP each get their own EWMA ratio, with the same windows as the rest of the app. Only running sessions count, and ForeCheck says so while the history is still shorter than the chronic window.
  6. The same GPS metrics are the load model for field sports such as football and rugby: distance as volume, metres per minute as intensity, high-speed and sprint distance and accelerations as high-intensity load. Ice hockey keeps its skating model.

Why

The speed zones are the absolute thresholds most used in football research. A systematic review found no international agreement on the exact values, so in ForeCheck they are a club setting, as is the acceleration threshold.

Limits, stated openly

Absolute speed zones treat every player the same. A fast player reaches the sprint zone more easily than a slow one, so compare a player mainly with himself.
